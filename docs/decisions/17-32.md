# Decisions 17 to 32

The full records; [the index](../decisions.md) lists every decision in one line.

17. **TAO wallet: read-only, by its public coldkey address, through the Taostats API** (2026-11-14). `GET /api/account/latest/v1` gives free, staked and total TAO; it's valued at Binance's TAO/USDT price and shown as a second source on the Portfolio page. The address and the Taostats key go in `.env`. Why: the owner's brief. Instead of: running a Bittensor node, or signing with the wallet, which nothing here needs.
18. *Superseded by 60 and 75:* **Trade page, first version: a watchlist of up to 10 coins on the left, the chart and AI signal in the middle, a limit buy or limit sell form on the right** (2026-11-14). The watchlist lives in the SQLite file (decision 13). The form prefills the limit price from the signal's suggested buy level, or from resistance for a sell; every order goes through the confirm step, the limits and the kill switch. A limit buy can still carry the smart trade's take-profit and stop-loss (decision 12), shown as an option under the form. Why: the owner's brief and mockup. Instead of: the smart trade ticket as the only way to trade, or market orders on this page.
19. *Superseded by 24, 60 and 75:* **Two pages, Portfolio and Trade, after the owner's mockup** (2026-11-14). Portfolio replaces the Dashboard; the kill switch joins the top bar's connection pills, since spec 10 keeps it always in view. Why: the owner's brief. Instead of: Dashboard and Trade terminal.
20. **At the owner's request, the agent may move keys from a file the owner names into `.env` with a script that never shows them** (2026-11-14). The script reports only what it found (how many values and of which kind), never a value; the agent still never reads, prints or copies a key, and the `.env` read rules stay in `.claude/settings.json`. Why: the owner asked the agent to set up their Binance key. Instead of: the owner pasting each key by hand (decision 2, which otherwise stands).
21. **The Trade page trades in one stablecoin, set by `DESK_QUOTE`: USDC for the owner** (2026-11-14). The watchlist, chart, signals, orders and limits all use it; the portfolio still values everything in US dollars through USDT prices. SPX isn't listed on Binance spot (it trades on Binance Alpha, which has no order API), so it can't be watched or traded here. Why: the owner trades only USDC pairs. Instead of: USDT, the template's default, which stays the default when the setting is missing.
22. **The Trade page's chart is TradingView's free Advanced Chart widget** (2026-11-14; Signal view, on Lightweight Charts, removed the same day at the owner's request, and the package with it). The widget is framed straight from `www.tradingview-widget.com`, the same page TradingView's embed script builds, so no third-party script runs on the desk's origin; the page's policy allows framing that one site. It shows TradingView's own data and tools, and can't draw the AI's buy level; the signal's levels stay in the analysis under the chart. Why: the owner wants the full TradingView experience. Instead of: TradingView's embed script on the page (a third-party script beside the order form, against code-standards 27), or Lightweight Charts alone.
23. **Several named watchlists, up to 20, each of up to 10 coins; the first is "Portfolio"** (2026-11-14). The picker shows each list with its coin count; the single watchlist from before moves into Portfolio on first run. Signals are kept per coin, so a coin on two lists shares one. Hidden from the portfolio: holdings worth under $5 or with no price (dust), always: the owner has no use for them; amounts of holdings under $10 round to 2 places. Why: the owner's requests. Instead of: one watchlist of 10.
24. *Partly superseded by 68:* **A folding side panel replaces the top bar's links; Portfolio is renamed Dashboard; DCA and Grid bots get placeholder pages** (2026-11-14). The panel copies the 3Commas layout the owner showed, room for the bots planned next; status pills and the kill switch move to its foot. The Dashboard's total leads with Australian dollars. Why: the owner's request. Instead of: top-bar links, which have no room for more pages.
25. **Coin logos come from CoinGecko through the api, kept in `local/icons/`** (2026-11-14). The api looks a ticker up once, takes the largest coin with exactly that ticker, downloads its logo and serves it from then on; the desk shows the coin's letters when there's none. Why: the owner wants each coin's own, current logo, and any coin added later gets one without a release. Instead of: the `cryptocurrency-icons` package (last updated 2022, missing PUMP, HYPE and other newer coins), or loading images from CoinGecko in the browser, which the content security policy blocks and which would tell CoinGecko the owner's IP for every page view.
26. **The Dashboard filters by source and keeps its table sleek** (2026-11-14). All, Binance or Wallet sets the total (in AUD and USD, with its 24h change), allocation, coins held, profit and the balances table; the 30-day line stays the whole account. The table rounds values and profit to whole dollars, prices and amounts by size (design-system 18); average cost uses the price's rounding, so cheap coins no longer show $0.00; how much of a holding the trades cover shows as a share, with the exact amounts on hover. Why: the owner's requests. Instead of: 2 places everywhere, or a filter on the table alone.
27. **The owner can enter an average cost for a holding with no trades here** (2026-11-14). The TAO wallet's coins, or coins deposited to Binance, have no trade history to cost. The Dashboard's Average cost cell offers "Add cost"; `PUT /cost` saves it (a positive number, a known source and ticker) in the `costs` table, each change goes in the audit log, and the entered cost stands for the whole holding, overriding any trades. Clearing it (empty) removes it. The PnL card is a summary per account (Binance, Wallet) in AUD, USD and percent; per-coin PnL stays in the table. The 30-day account value box is off the Dashboard for now; the api still records each day's value. Why: the owner bought TAO off Binance and wants its PnL. Instead of: importing wallet history from Taostats, which knows transfers but not prices paid.
28. **Key levels sit in a quiet ladder over the TradingView chart** (2026-11-14). The market reply carries this month's, week's and day's opens and the previous week's and day's highs and lows, from Binance's 1M, 1w and 1d candles (three more read-only calls when a coin is picked; none if Binance refuses, and the chart still shows), plus the existing support and resistance from the last 30 4h candles. The desk lists them highest first with the price in its place and each one's distance from it, above in `down`, below in `up`. Why: the owner wants week and day opens and support and resistance without a crowded chart. Instead of: lines on the chart, which the embedded TradingView chart can't take from the desk, or preloaded TradingView indicators, whose embed IDs aren't documented for the free widget.
29. **Australian dollars use a live USDT rate, with the ECB's daily rate as fallback** (2026-11-14). The portfolio's AUD figures convert at CoinGecko's USDT→AUD price (6 places; a reply outside 0.8 to 3 is ignored), and at the ECB's daily USD→AUD rate when CoinGecko can't be had. Why: the ECB rate is the previous working day's, so the Binance figure in AUD ran about 0.5% under Binance's own, which converts live. Small gaps remain: Binance uses its own rate and prices a few coins the desk can't (no USDT pair). Instead of: the ECB rate alone, or Binance's AUD/USDT pair, which is thinly traded and was 7% off.
30. **The Dashboard shows money in AUD, USD, INR or BTC, picked by a switch** (2026-11-14). The api sends `total.rates`, units per US dollar: AUD and INR from CoinGecko's live USDT price (each falling back to the ECB's daily rate on its own), BTC from Binance's BTC/USDT price. A switch beside the source filter converts every money figure on the Dashboard (the total and its 24h change, the account values, stablecoins, PnL, and the table's prices, values, average costs and PnL); open orders and recent trades stay in their pair's quote, as Binance records them. The choice is kept per browser, AUD until picked; a currency with no rate is greyed out. INR uses Indian digit grouping. Why: the owner's request. Instead of: AUD with USD under it everywhere.
31. **USDC deposits into Binance show by month for the financial year, in AUD** (2026-11-14). The api reads Binance's USDC deposit history (`/sapi/v1/capital/deposit/hisrec`, read-only, in 90-day windows from 1 July) and values each deposit at what the owner paid where that's recorded (`deposit_paid` in the local SQLite, keyed by the exact USDC amount, which the sending transfer and the Binance deposit share; set through `PUT /deposits/paid`, each change in the audit log), and otherwise at the amount times the ECB's US dollar to AUD rate on the day it landed (USDC as a dollar; a weekend takes Friday's rate). A small Dashboard card beside PnL shows the year's AUD and USDC totals, the average A$ per USDC, and a mini bar a month from July to June; picking a month shows its total and how many deposits were estimated. The owner's past amounts were entered into their local database from their own records and are never in the repo (known-mistakes 7). Why: the owner funds Binance with USDC bought elsewhere with AUD, and wants each financial year's deposits in AUD. Instead of: reading their mailbox at run time, or the day's rate alone where the price paid is known.
32. **The wallet line shows the TAO total, with unclaimed root rewards in brackets** (2026-11-14). Taostats' `root_basket_claimable_tao` is the root-staking reward waiting to be claimed; the Dashboard shows "TAO <staked + free + rewards, cut to 1 place> τ · incl. <rewards> τ in baskets", with staked, free and rewards on hover. The rewards count as held: the wallet's TAO amount, value and allocation include them, and an entered average cost covers only the TAO bought, so the rewards add to PnL at no cost. Why: the owner wants one total and to see what's claimable. Instead of: staked plus free on one line, without the rewards.
