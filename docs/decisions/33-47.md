The full records; [the index](../decisions.md) lists every decision in one line.

33. **A stablecoin can carry an average cost the owner enters, and percentages show 1 decimal place** (2026-11-14). USDC was left without a cost; its cost is now entered like the TAO wallet's (decision 27), estimated from the year's deposits as the AUD paid converted to US dollars at each deposit day's rate, divided by the USDC that arrived. The desk's PnL stays in US dollars, so it shows USDC near break-even; the Australian-dollar gain from a weaker dollar isn't counted as PnL. Percentages everywhere round to 1 place. The average cost editor closes when focus leaves it. Why: the owner's requests. Instead of: no cost for stablecoins, or 2-place percentages.
34. **Average cost counts only trades from the start of FY 2026-27** (2026-11-14). Trades before `DESK_COST_FROM` (a date, Sydney time; 1 Jul 2026 by default) are left out of each coin's average cost and recent trades. Why: TAO bought on Binance and withdrawn to the wallet in earlier years still counted as held there, at an older, higher average; the owner wants the current year only. Instead of: replaying Binance's withdrawal history, whose calls are costly against Binance's limits, or counting every trade ever made.
35. **BNB, the fee token, costs the average of the year's BNB buys, for all that's held** (2026-11-14). Binance takes trading fees in BNB, so BNB leaves without a sale and the year's trades can't account for what's held (they net to nothing while 0.05 BNB remains). Its average cost is the year's buys' total paid over the BNB bought, applied to the whole holding; sales and fees don't change it, as with any average cost. Why: the owner wants BNB's PnL for FY 2026-27. Instead of: no PnL for BNB, or replaying every fee across every pair.
36. *Superseded by 63 and 64:* **The Dashboard's top is eight compact widgets, four a row, including the Crypto Fear & Greed Index** (2026-11-14). The balances table gains an Invested column (the amount each cost covers, at that cost) and a totals row for invested, value and PnL; coin amounts are cut, not rounded, so the wallet's TAO reads the same everywhere. Why: the owner wants compact, useful widgets and the totals. Instead of: three tall columns, or a market-data subscription.
37. **DCA bots: a Bollinger Band entry, a ladder of limit buys and a limit take profit, switched on by the owner** (2026-11-14). A bot watches one pair in the trading stablecoin. When a closed 15m, 1h or 4h candle closes below the lower Bollinger Band (20, 2 by default), it places a base order and up to 20 DCA orders at once, as resting limit buys: each a deviation % below the last (the steps grow by the deviation multiplier, the sizes by the DCA size multiplier). Each time one fills, the desk replaces the limit sell for everything held at take profit % over the average price. When that sells, the deal closes, its open buys are cancelled and the bot waits for the next signal, or switches off, as set. A base order that doesn't fill within one candle is cancelled. There is no stop loss in this version. Bots are created switched off; switching one on is the owner's confirm for every order it may place, stated in words with its total. That replaces the per-order confirm for bots only (spec 10). Every order stays within the per-order limit, bot buys count towards the daily limit, and the kill switch turns every bot off and cancels their open buys, leaving take-profit sells in place. A Binance error pauses the bot. The wizard shows the ladder at today's price, the limit checks and a backtest on up to 1,000 past candles (fees 0.1% a side; take profit checked before a candle's fills, so it errs cautious), with presets for steady, balanced and deep-dip ladders. Why: the owner's request, after 3Commas', Binance's and Bybit's DCA bots; the backtest and presets follow 3Commas and Gainium. Instead of: market orders, an AI-driven entry, several pairs per bot, max active DCA orders, or a stop loss.
38. **Grid bots: a range of limit orders that buys low and sells one line up, switched on by the owner** (2026-11-14). A grid splits a lower to upper price on one pair into 2 to 100 grids, geometric (each grid the same %) or arithmetic (the same price step), and divides the investment evenly across them. On switching on, a limit buy goes on each line below the line nearest the price. With buy first (the default, as on Binance), there is also a limit buy at today's price for the coins each sell above it needs; without it, the grid starts with buys only. When a buy fills, a limit sell goes one line up; when a sell fills, a limit buy goes one line down, and the round trip's profit is recorded. An optional stop-below price cancels its orders and keeps the coins. There are no market orders, so no stop-loss sale. Switching it off cancels all its orders. The kill switch cancels its buys and leaves its sells, which only take risk away. Each order stays within the per-order limit, and grid buys count towards the daily limit; a buy that today's limit can't take waits for tomorrow. A Binance error pauses the grid. The wizard starts on the last 14 days' low and high and shows profit per grid after fees, the opening buys, the limit checks, a week's chart with the lines, and a backtest over about 41 days of hourly candles against simply holding the coin. Why: the owner's request, after Binance's, Pionex's and Bitsgap's spot grids. Instead of: an initial market buy, trailing up or down, or a stop-loss that sells.
39. **Ladder buys on the smart trade, and a switch to hide the TradingView chart** (2026-11-14). The order form's limit buy has One buy or Ladder. A ladder places 2 to 20 limit buys evenly spaced from a top to a bottom price, the total split equally or weighted 1, 2, 3... towards the lower prices, each amount rounded down to the pair's step. Each rung is its own smart trade (decision 12) with the same optional take-profit and stop-loss, which go to Binance as one OCO order once that rung fills. The api works the rungs out again and checks the whole ladder before sending anything: Binance's minimum, the per-order limit for each rung and the daily limit for the total, so a ladder is placed whole or not at all. One confirm states every rung, the total and the average price if all fill. Each rung's Binance id ends with its number, so a double submit places nothing new. The Trade page's Hide chart button folds the TradingView chart away, remembered per browser. Why: the owner's request; splitting a buy across levels to improve the average entry is how 3Commas' SmartTrade users ladder. Instead of: one combined take-profit on the average (that's the DCA bot, decision 37), or split take-profit targets, which can come next.

41. **A smart trade is a one-off DCA deal, made by a four-step New trade; key levels keep only what an entry needs** (2026-11-14). New trade walks Entry (a suggested price: the AI buy level, the nearest support within 3%, or today's price), DCA orders (optional, from the coin's 30-day preset), Take profit (a toggle; % over the average, moved as each buy fills) and Stop loss (a toggle; a price, 3% under the last DCA order to start). Each field says why. It runs on the DCA engine (decision 37) with an entry price instead of a signal: the deal opens at once, its entry waits for its price without expiring, and when the deal ends the trade is finished (reference TR-n). Under the stop loss, the watcher cancels the deal's orders and sells what's held at market, as does the owner's Close; both need the desk running, as a moving take profit already does. The chart beside it shows the entry, each DCA order and both exits, with the reward, risk and reward to risk if all fill. A plain limit Sell stays. The single-buy form, the ladder (decision 39, now DCA orders) and the separate limits panel are gone; the limits are one line with Edit. The levels strip over the chart shows at most the nearest ceiling and floor (from resistance, support and yesterday's and last week's highs and lows) and the week and day opens, within 10%, in plain words. Why: the owner's request. Instead of: one buy or a ladder with fixed exits, or every level the candles give.

45. **The TAO wallet reading is kept: reused for a minute, and standing in for six hours when Taostats refuses** (2026-11-15). 46. **The AI panel is a plan for the owner's own position: numbers from code, choices and words from the model** (2026-11-15). The panel shows: a position strip (amount, value, average cost, PnL, share of the portfolio, or the cash available when not held); an action (Buy, Add, Hold, Trim, Sell or Wait) with its confidence and last run's, and one for the short term, the swing and the long term; a plan (a buy or add zone, don't chase above, an invalidation on a daily close with the next level, a trim zone), each zone with Prefill, which opens the smart trade form at that price and sends nothing; a what-if of adding (new average, the rise to break even before and after, the share of the portfolio, cash left), its amount editable and starting at the per-trade budget; why (one for, one against, what would change its mind); context only when it matters (over the cap a coin; a sale that would realise a loss, with a neutral note that the Australian financial year ends 30 June, never tax advice); up to three triggers to watch, each with Set alert; and when it ran, what changed, and its record (each signal a day later: Buy or Add right if the price rose, Sell or Trim if it fell, Hold or Wait if it moved under 3%). Everything numeric is worked out in code (`api/plan.py`, `api/advisor.py`, and `desk/src/plan.ts` for the editable what-if): support and resistance are daily and weekly swing lows and highs, 0.5% or more from the price and 1.5% or more apart, as S1 to S3 and R1 to R3; indicators on 4h, 1D and 1W (RSI, 20, 50 and 200 averages, trend); volume against the month; funding and open interest from Binance's public futures data where there is a perpetual; Fear & Greed; the last signal. The model gets these and the owner's position (amount, average cost, invested, value, PnL, share, cash, open orders' side, price and amount) and nothing else private: no keys, account ids or order ids. It returns JSON against a schema built for the call, naming levels only by this call's ids; code maps ids to prices, swaps any id in its text for the price, and holds the plan to the owner's risk rules (max 25% a coin, A$500 a trade, max loss 20% a position by default, editable): no Buy or Add over the cap, nothing sold that isn't held. An answer out of shape is refused and the last good plan stays. Price alerts are kept in SQLite and checked by the watcher every 30 seconds. Level chips over the chart read their distance in words, without colour. Why: the owner's brief. Instead of: the model working out numbers, or a plan that ignores the position. This replaces decision 16's rule that the signal never sees balances.
47. **An order sent without an answer is looked up, never assumed lost; one lock for everything that sends orders** (2026-11-15, from the branch review). A Binance call that times out (status 0) may still have placed the order. A bot's or grid's order stays "placing" and the next step finds it by its client id: open if Binance has it, back to wanted or cancelled if it never arrived. A smart trade's buy that times out is looked up the same way before it's called cancelled. A limit sell checks Binance for its client id before it's sent, so a double submit sells once. A deal's stop-loss or Close sale at market is recorded under its own client id before it's sent and never sent twice; the deal ends once the sale has settled. A grid that still has orders out (after a pause or the kill switch) can't be started again until it's stopped, so a second opening can't land on top of the first. The 30-second watcher and the owner's clicks share one lock around every action that sends orders. The AI plan no longer falls back to another model, so "model" and the spend are always Opus 5.5's. Why: the branch review found each of these could place, lose or double an order. Instead of: treating every error as "not placed".
