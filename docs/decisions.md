37. **DCA bots: a Bollinger Band entry, a ladder of limit buys and a limit take profit, switched on by the owner** (2026-11-14). A bot watches one pair in the trading stablecoin. When a closed 15m, 1h or 4h candle closes below the lower Bollinger Band (20, 2 by default), it places a base order and up to 20 DCA orders at once, as resting limit buys: each a deviation % below the last (the steps grow by the deviation multiplier, the sizes by the DCA size multiplier). Each time one fills, the desk replaces the limit sell for everything held at take profit % over the average price. When that sells, the deal closes, its open buys are cancelled and the bot waits for the next signal, or switches off, as set. A base order that doesn't fill within one candle is cancelled. There is no stop loss in this version. Bots are created switched off; switching one on is the owner's confirm for every order it may place, stated in words with its total. That replaces the per-order confirm for bots only (spec 10). Every order stays within the per-order limit, bot buys count towards the daily limit, and the kill switch turns every bot off and cancels their open buys, leaving take-profit sells in place. A Binance error pauses the bot. The wizard shows the ladder at today's price, the limit checks and a backtest on up to 1,000 past candles (fees 0.1% a side; take profit checked before a candle's fills, so it errs cautious), with presets for steady, balanced and deep-dip ladders. Why: the owner's request, after 3Commas', Binance's and Bybit's DCA bots; the backtest and presets follow 3Commas and Gainium. Instead of: market orders, an AI-driven entry, several pairs per bot, max active DCA orders, or a stop loss.
38. **Grid bots: a range of limit orders that buys low and sells one line up, switched on by the owner** (2026-11-14). A grid splits a lower to upper price on one pair into 2 to 100 grids, geometric (each grid the same %) or arithmetic (the same price step), and divides the investment evenly across them. On switching on, a limit buy goes on each line below the line nearest the price. With buy first (the default, as on Binance), there is also a limit buy at today's price for the coins each sell above it needs; without it, the grid starts with buys only. When a buy fills, a limit sell goes one line up; when a sell fills, a limit buy goes one line down, and the round trip's profit is recorded. An optional stop-below price cancels its orders and keeps the coins. There are no market orders, so no stop-loss sale. Switching it off cancels all its orders. The kill switch cancels its buys and leaves its sells, which only take risk away. Each order stays within the per-order limit, and grid buys count towards the daily limit; a buy that today's limit can't take waits for tomorrow. A Binance error pauses the grid. The wizard starts on the last 14 days' low and high and shows profit per grid after fees, the opening buys, the limit checks, a week's chart with the lines, and a backtest over about 41 days of hourly candles against simply holding the coin. Why: the owner's request, after Binance's, Pionex's and Bitsgap's spot grids. Instead of: an initial market buy, trailing up or down, or a stop-loss that sells.
39. **Ladder buys on the smart trade, and a switch to hide the TradingView chart** (2026-11-14). The order form's limit buy has One buy or Ladder. A ladder places 2 to 20 limit buys evenly spaced from a top to a bottom price, the total split equally or weighted 1, 2, 3... towards the lower prices, each amount rounded down to the pair's step. Each rung is its own smart trade (decision 12) with the same optional take-profit and stop-loss, which go to Binance as one OCO order once that rung fills. The api works the rungs out again and checks the whole ladder before sending anything: Binance's minimum, the per-order limit for each rung and the daily limit for the total, so a ladder is placed whole or not at all. One confirm states every rung, the total and the average price if all fill. Each rung's Binance id ends with its number, so a double submit places nothing new. The Trade page's Hide chart button folds the TradingView chart away, remembered per browser. Why: the owner's request; splitting a buy across levels to improve the average entry is how 3Commas' SmartTrade users ladder. Instead of: one combined take-profit on the average (that's the DCA bot, decision 37), or split take-profit targets, which can come next.
40. **Bots work like a consumer app: presets from the last 30 days, a real chart with the levels, references for everything, and a page per bot** (2026-11-14).   A bot is named after its pair. DCA bots can run 1 to 5 deals at once, each starting on a later signal, and the confirm states the total. After a take profit a DCA bot always waits for the next signal (the next-deal-or-stop choice is gone). Every bot, deal and order has a reference shown in the app and written to the audit log: DCA-1, D-12 and D-12-3 or D-12-TP; GRID-2 and GRID-2-40; ST-7 for a smart trade. Text shown to the owner carries no project notes such as decision numbers. Why: the owner's requests and 3Commas screens. Instead of: free-text names and coins, hand-filled settings, or an SVG sketch of the levels.
41. **A smart trade is a one-off DCA deal, made by a four-step New trade; key levels keep only what an entry needs** (2026-11-14). The single-buy form, the ladder (decision 39, now DCA orders) and the separate limits panel are gone; the limits are one line with Edit. The levels strip over the chart shows at most the nearest ceiling and floor (from resistance, support and yesterday's and last week's highs and lows) and the week and day opens, within 10%, in plain words. Why: the owner's request. Instead of: one buy or a ladder with fixed exits, or every level the candles give.
